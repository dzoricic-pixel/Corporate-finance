Historical Overview

  • Model 3 "Production Hell" Era (Late 2017 – Mid 2018): Tesla faced severe operational bottlenecks scaling automated manufacturing for the Model 3, causing heavy cash burn (~$750M in a single quarter) and fears of insolvency before reaching sustained positive cash flow in Q3 2018.
  • Investor Push to Oust Musk (2018): Mounting cash burn and delivery delays sparked activist shareholder proposals urging board members to strip Elon Musk of his authority. Following his controversial "funding secured" tweet, the U.S. SEC required him to step down as Chairman (while remaining CEO). See CBS News coverage: "Elon Musk to step down as chair of Tesla board, settles with SEC for $20 million".
  • Price Cuts & Margin Squeeze (Early 2024 – Early 2025): Escalating EV market competition and high interest rates forced vehicle price cuts, reducing profit margins and driving Q1 2024 Free Cash Flow into negative territory, which raised concerns over prioritizing AI and Robotaxi spending over core EV profitability.
  • Evolution of Musk's Stake: Musk maintained an approximate 20% to 22% stake during the 2018 production crisis (granting roughly 26% total voting power). His direct ownership fell to ~13% in 2022 to fund his acquisition of Twitter (now X), before returning to ~20% following the re-ratification of his 2018 CEO compensation package.
  • Voting Control Objectives: Musk has publicly expressed an ambition to reach around 25% voting control in Tesla to secure strategic direction over its artificial intelligence and robotics initiatives.
